## Scope and core requirements

Research a real technology/system that relies on electromagnetics and explain how course theory accounts for its operation or design. The textbook is *Fundamentals of Applied Electromagnetics*, Ulaby & Ravaioli, 8th edition.

- Make explicit connections to **at least two specific textbook concepts** for the highest rubric level.
- Show relevant equations and correctly apply them to the application; a descriptive overview alone does not meet the strongest rubric levels.
- Include **at least one quantitative analysis**, state assumptions, label graphs with units, interpret results, and check reasonableness.
- Explain diagrams and define terminology not covered in class.
- Include an **ambiguity interpretation**: identify a specific issue without one obvious answer, explain alternatives considered, justify the final approach using technical evidence and constraints, and reflect on how well the team handled it.
- Divide work equitably. Research, calculations/simulations, slides, and speaking all count as contributions.

## Material relevant to maglev in the project brief

The brief explicitly offers **Electromagnets and Magnetic Levitation** as an example. It points to Chapter 5 magnetostatics, Ampère's law, Biot–Savart law, the solenoid field approximation, Lorentz force, magnetic energy, and the balance between gravity and magnetic lift.

Its example work includes estimating a coil's field and lifting force, varying coil turns/current in a small electromagnet experiment, or doing a magnetostatic simulation. These are suggestions, not a required build. The brief also offers microwave standing waves and supercapacitors as alternative example topics.

## Suggested presentation structure from the brief

1. Title, team name, members.
2. Introduction/background: application and why it matters.
3. How it works: intuitive explanation and labeled system diagram.
4. Key EM concepts: specific course theory and equations.
5. Application of theory and quantitative analysis/simulation.
6. Results/discussion with labeled, interpreted evidence.
7. Ambiguity interpretation: alternatives, decision, justification, reflection.
8. References (recommended in the brief; its outline labels them optional).

The exact slide titles and outline are not mandatory. Rehearse to fit the total time including Q&A.
